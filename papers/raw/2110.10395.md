\vspace{-0.5em}
\noindent \textbf{Novelty (\red{R1-Q1, R3-Q1}):} The proposed face completion (FC) method, \underline{\ourmethod{}}, has three parts, (i) disentangling 2D image into factors such as 3D pose, 3D shape, albedo and illumination, (ii) enforcing symmetry in UV albedo, and (iii) iterative refinement of FC through progressively more accurate 3D pose estimation. \underline{UVGAN [7]} on the other hand, (i) completes self-occluded texture only from a fully-visible face image, instead of completing partially visible faces, (ii) unlike \ourmethod{}, does not disentangle texture further into albedo and illumination, (iii) does not impose symmetry prior on UV texture, and (iv) no iterative refinement. Since no source code or pretrained model of UVGAN is available, we evaluate these differences by reformulating UVGAN to complete missing texture and train it for FC. Fig.\ref{fig:uvgan_comp} shows numerical and qualitative comparison. \ourmethod{} \emph{significantly} outperforms UVGAN on the CelebA dataset. Introducing symmetry loss (SYM) in UVGAN-Sym hurts performance since, unlike UV-albedo, UV-texture is not inherently symmetric in faces. Completion on albedo (IL) instead improves results in \ourmethod-NoIR. Lastly, iterative refinement (IR) further improves completion. For the above reasons, we argue that \ourmethod{} introduces many novelties over UVGAN [7]. \underline{SymmFCNet [17]} uses symmetry in 2D only. Results from author's pretrained model were very poor.

% We answer this in light of two of our main insights: (i) using 3D priors leads to geometrically and visually better face completion (FC) than 2D based approaches, and (ii) symmetry in UV is a strong prior for FC. \textbf{UVGAN [7]} completes self-occluded texture from a fully-visible face image, instead of completing partial face. Unlike us, UVGAN [7] does not do complete 3D disentanglement into albedo and illumination and hence, refrain from using symmetry loss. Since their source code is not publicly available, we evaluate these design choices by reformulating and training UVGAN [7] for FC by completing missing texture after 3D factorization. Fig.\ref{fig:uvgan_comp} summarises the results with a visual comparison. Our full method (\ourmethod) achieves better PSNR and LPIPS metrics than the UVGAN [7] on the CelebA [21] dataset. Introducing symmetry loss (SYM) in UVGAN-Sym worsens the metrics because of the variations in illumination, however complete disentanglement (IL) improves performance in \ourmethod-NoIR. The proposed iterative finetuning (IF) further improves completion in the second iteration (\ourmethod). \underline{SymmFCNet [17]} uses symmetry in 2D, but gives suboptimal results (using their pretrained model).

\noindent\textbf{Special Masks and Detail Generation:} \red{R1-Q3, R3-Q2, R3-Q3} \ourmethod{} inpaints regions that are included in the underlying 3D face model. We chose BFM [23] due to its wide support. But, BFM, (i) does not model the inner-mouth, so restricts inpainting in the inner-mouth and background, and (ii) has a coarse mesh in some parts, especially around the eyes, leading to low-resolution around those regions. \emph{However, these limitations of the 3D model do not invalidate our overall approach in terms of leveraging geometric and photometric consistency}. We can improve coverage and density by swapping with a more detailed 3D model like the Universal Head Model (UHM). \underline{Real occlusions (\red{R1-Q4}):} We show examples of real-world occlusions in Fig. \ref{fig:real_world_compare}. Notice that, in challenging cases of pose, illumination \etc, the baselines tend to generate blurry and asymmetric face completions, whereas \ourmethod{} does more realistic de-occlusion. \underline{Accuracy-generality trade-off:} In general, SOTA 2D based methods excel in generality rather than object-based accuracy, but on structured objects like faces, the geometric and photometric accuracy of inpainting is important where 3D methods like ours excel.
